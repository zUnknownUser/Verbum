## Editorial boundaries

- Faith: Mark 9 is not presented as a guarantee of healing or a basis for blaming a person’s continuing suffering. The discussion of prayer does not make a textual-critical claim about the wording concerning fasting. Hebrews includes the delay between trust and complete fulfillment. James’s argument begins with unmet material need; the study does not pretend to settle every later debate about justification.
- Covenant: Jeremiah’s original addressees remain Israel and Judah. Luke’s use of new-covenant language is identified as a later reading in its own Gospel setting. No claim of automatic replacement of Israel, one compulsory sacramental theory or an asserted reconstruction of Genesis 15’s ancient ritual is introduced.
- Worship: the calf episode is distinguished from its claimed religious language; naming the Lord does not erase the narrative’s judgment. John 4 retains its Jewish context and is not reduced to a preference for musical styles, buildings, emotion or intellect.
- Obedience: the manna instructions vary with the day. The study distinguishes contextual instructions from universal slogans, and Jesus’ words are not silently equated with every demand by a human leader.
- Holiness: ritual preparation, divine presence and ethical conduct are related without being treated as interchangeable. First Peter reuses scriptural holiness language; Romans 12 is labeled a thematic parallel, not a direct quotation of Leviticus 19. Moses’ inability to enter in Exodus 40 is not attributed to an offense the passage does not name.
- No modern archaeological location, historical date, denominational consensus or scholarly authorship theory is asserted. No proprietary commentary was copied.
